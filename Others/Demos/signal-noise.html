01

Claims

Statements that assert something about the world, especially when they use strong certainty or concrete propositions.

02

Facts

Language containing dates, quantities, measurements, named records, or other concrete markers. This does not independently verify them.

03

Opinions

Interpretive or evaluative language signaled by phrases such as “I think,” “better,” “should,” or “in my view.”

04

Questions

Interrogative sentences. Questions are preserved as their own layer because uncertainty can itself be meaningful.

05

Emotion

Words carrying strong affect, urgency, praise, anger, fear, excitement, contempt, or alarm.

06

Filler

Hedges, discourse markers, repetition, and low-information framing that can soften or surround the central statement.
